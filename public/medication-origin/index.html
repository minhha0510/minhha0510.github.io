WHY LOOK BACK?

Good questions
often begin with
something noticed.

An effect in a culture dish. A signal in the body. A pattern in patients. What did researchers notice, and what did they make of it?

This atlas follows those starting points through to established medicine classes. It looks at the observation and the human design that followed: new structures, altered activity, better selectivity, and ways to turn a lead into a treatment.

It is part of a broader interest in learning from biological and clinical observations, and in judging the evidence well. These histories invite that investigation; they do not, on their own, measure the success rate of a discovery strategy.

Methodology

How we counted the 60% +

The 69 histories include 30 new therapeutic clues and 39 contributions to refinement or inherited therapeutic rationale. Each class counts once. Six human links remain unresolved; 40 are not established in the available records.

This is the author's 8 November 2026 interpretation, with source and arithmetic checks. Independent historical review of this additional classification is pending. The broader origin filters also include laboratory and animal observations.

01 / SELECT

150 commonly prescribed medication groups each from the US and England: 226 after combining the lists, grouped into 115 classes.

02 / TRACE

Follow each class through original experiments, discoverer accounts and historical sources.

03 / CHECK

Link claims to their sources, show evidence limits and retain disagreements. All 115 histories have author source checks.

Sources and review scope +

US dispensing data cover 2024; England data cover 2025/26. Classes can contain distinct lineages. The linked histories distinguish observed clues, later design and uncertainty.

Independent review of the original atlas covered metadata and numbers for all 115 classes, 12 deeper targets and source-gap samples in seven classes. It did not independently verify every cited passage.
